# Interview Guide: Animal Rescue Volunteer Group

Goal: discover recurring coordination decisions without drifting into volunteer shift matching, which is outside the project scope. Duration: 20 minutes.

## Opening and consent

1. May I take structured notes without recording personal details?
2. I am studying coordination friction, not evaluating individual volunteers. Is that acceptable?
3. Do not share names, addresses, phone numbers, medical details, or private case information during this interview.

## Current workflow

1. Think of the last week: which three things required repeated follow-up in the group?
2. For one completed case, where did the commitment first appear and how was completion confirmed?
3. Which decisions had a clear owner, and which appeared to belong to everyone?
4. What information was repeated because people could not find the earlier message?
5. Which low-risk actions could have proceeded with a visible undo option?

## Authority and interruption

6. Which decisions can one coordinator make, which need two people, and which need the whole group?
7. Which actions are reversible within 24 hours?
8. Which actions involve money, public reputation, animal welfare, or personal safety?
9. How often is it acceptable to privately ask the same volunteer for a decision?
10. What should happen when no one responds within 24 hours?

## Evidence and outcome

11. How do you know a task is actually closed rather than merely acknowledged?
12. What would a useful Sunday summary contain on one screen?
13. What would make an automated coordinator feel intrusive?
14. If a one-week pilot becomes possible, who can authorize a locally redacted export?
15. Would someone be willing to review and approve one exact quotation after the pilot?

## Close

Summarize the observed workflow back to the participant. Ask them to correct it. Do not request an export until scope, consent, retention, and deletion are agreed.
